\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ with $y_i\in\{1,\dots,k\}$, and let
$\mathcal{M}=\{f_1,\dots,f_M\}$ be a fixed library of learning algorithms with
a~priori hyperparameters (Table~\ref{tab:zoo}). NEXUS has three stages, all of
which use only $\mathcal{D}$.

\paragraph{1. Cross-fitting and bagging.}
$\mathcal{D}$ is split into $K$ stratified folds $I_1,\dots,I_K$ ($K{=}5$). For every
algorithm $m$ and fold $j$ we fit $\hat f_{m}^{(-j)}$ on $\mathcal{D}\setminus I_j$
and record out-of-fold (OOF) class probabilities
$\hat p_m(x_i)=\hat f_m^{(-j)}(x_i)$ for $i\in I_j$. At test time the $K$ fold models
are averaged, $\bar f_m(x)=\frac1K\sum_j \hat f_m^{(-j)}(x)$, so no model is ever
refit on the full data. The OOF matrix gives every algorithm an honest held-out
estimate on all $n$ training points at no extra cost.

\paragraph{2. Greedy ensemble selection.}
Following \citet{caruana2004ensemble}, we build a weighted average
$\hat p_w=\sum_m w_m \hat p_m$ by forward selection with replacement: starting from
an empty bag, for $T{=}50$ iterations we add the algorithm whose inclusion most
reduces the OOF log loss of the running average, and set $w_m$ to the fraction of
iterations in which $m$ was chosen. The weights lie on the simplex and are
typically sparse; the constraint to convex combinations of probabilities is a
strong regulariser compared with a learned meta-model
\citep{caruana2006getting}, which we test directly against stacking in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{3. Temperature calibration.}
Averaging probabilistic predictions tends to under-disperse them. We fit a
single temperature $\tau$ by minimising the OOF log loss of
$\hat p_{w,\tau}(x)_c \propto \hat p_w(x)_c^{1/\tau}$ over $\tau\in[0.05,20]$
\citep{guo2017calibration}, and apply it to the test-time mixture.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
$\hat p(x)_c \propto \big(\sum_m w_m \bar f_m(x)_c\big)^{1/\tau}$. Models with $w_m=0$ are
never evaluated.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
NEXUS is a scikit-learn compatible estimator \citep{pedregosa2011scikit}. Tree
models receive ordinal-encoded categoricals with missing values left as NaN;
linear, $k$-NN and MLP models receive median-imputed, standardized numerics with
missing-value indicators and one-hot categoricals (at most 32 levels). Folds
where a rare class is absent are handled by fitting on the classes present and
assigning zero probability to the rest; $K$ shrinks automatically if the rarest
class has fewer than $K$ members. Total cost is $K\cdot M$ model fits.
