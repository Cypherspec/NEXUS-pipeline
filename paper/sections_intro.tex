\section{Introduction}

Computational drug discovery runs on a deep software stack. RDKit parses the
structures, scikit-learn or PyTorch fits the models, SciPy supplies the statistics,
and a dozen smaller packages fill the gaps. The stack is excellent and nobody should
rewrite it for production work. But it also means that a practitioner can run a
screening campaign end to end without once being forced to confront what a Murcko
scaffold is, where an aromaticity model draws its line, or what exactly a confidence
interval from a Gaussian process guarantees. The abstractions are good enough to be
used without being understood, and that is usually a virtue.

NEXUS is the opposite exercise. It implements the pipeline against NumPy alone ---
\codeLines{} lines of library code, \testLines{} lines of tests, \nTests{} tests ---
specifically so that every modelling decision has to be made explicitly and can
therefore be checked. SMILES parsing, ring perception and Hückel aromaticity;
Bemis--Murcko scaffolds \cite{bemis1996properties}; Morgan fingerprints
\cite{rogers2010extended} and Ertl TPSA \cite{ertl2000fast}; a tape-based
reverse-mode autodiff engine and a directed message-passing network
\cite{yang2019analyzing}; Gaussian-process active learning; split-conformal
prediction \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution}; Vietoris--Rips persistent
homology \cite{edelsbrunner2002topological}; Ollivier--Ricci curvature
\cite{ollivier2009ricci}; Jarzynski, Crooks and BAR free-energy estimators
\cite{jarzynski1997nonequilibrium,crooks1999entropy,bennett1976efficient}; a discrete
denoising diffusion model for molecular graphs
\cite{austin2021structured,vignac2023digress}; and a Gene Ontology layer with
information-theoretic semantic similarity \cite{ashburner2000gene,resnik1999semantic}.

\paragraph{What this paper claims.}
Not that these implementations are faster or more accurate than the established
ones --- they are neither. The claim is narrower and, we think, more useful: that
writing them out and validating each against an external reference exposes failures
that a stack of trusted packages would have hidden. Three such failures are
documented here. A scaffold splitter silently handed the validation set zero
molecules on one dataset. A graph generator scored below its own untrained baseline
because 83.7\% of atom pairs are ``no bond'' and the denoiser had learned to say so.
A conformal interval attained exactly its guaranteed coverage while being the same
width for every molecule, which is correct and nearly useless. None of these would
have produced an error message.

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.}
There is no state-of-the-art result here. Where a baseline beats the neural model,
the table says so. Where an improvement fails to improve, the negative result is
reported with the same prominence as the positive ones (Section~\ref{sec:negative}).
Hyperparameters selected on a reported metric are flagged as such.
