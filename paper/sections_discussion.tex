\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.} The results in \cref{sec:results}
show where a small, fully specified ensemble of standard learners
stands relative to strong single models and to a mature AutoML system under an
equal compute budget. The development study (\cref{sec:dev}) and the CC18
ablations point to the same mechanisms: averaging over cross-fitted models and
selecting a sparse convex combination of diverse learners do most of the work,
a light second stacking layer adds a consistent but modest gain, and further
engineering (larger portfolios, alternative weight optimisers, repeated
cross-fitting) yields little on data of this size.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Scope.} We evaluate \NTasks{} of the 72 CC18 tasks: datasets above
12{,}000 rows or 300 features were excluded by a rule fixed in advance for a
4-core CPU budget. Conclusions may not transfer to large or high-dimensional
data, to regression, or to GPU-accelerated deep models.
\item \emph{Compute budget for AutoGluon.} AutoGluon receives the same wall-clock
budget as NEXUS on a single core, typically a few minutes. With hour-scale
budgets, as in the AutoML Benchmark \citep{gijsbers2024amlb}, AutoGluon can
train more models and its results would likely improve. Our comparison
answers a specific question: what each approach achieves with equal, modest compute.
\item \emph{Missing competitors.} We could not include TabPFN
\citep{hollmann2025tabpfn}, whose weights require a license acceptance not
available in our environment; it is known to be strong on small data and is the
most important comparison to add.
\item \emph{Single run per fold.} Each fold uses one seed. Variability across
folds and tasks is captured by our tests, but not seed variance within a fold.
\item \emph{Novelty.} Each component of NEXUS (bagging, ensemble selection,
stacking, temperature scaling) is established. Our contribution is a careful,
held-out-design characterisation of their combination, not a new learning
algorithm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
NEXUS combines bagged cross-fitting, a light stacking layer, greedy convex
ensemble selection and temperature scaling into a deterministic,
search-free classifier. We designed it on held-out development datasets, froze
it, and evaluated it once on \NTasks{} OpenML-CC18 tasks with official splits,
equal-budget AutoGluon, and rigorous multi-dataset statistics. Code, cached
predictions and a one-command reproduction script are released with the paper.
