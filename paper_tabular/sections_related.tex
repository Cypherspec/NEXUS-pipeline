\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Ensemble selection and stacking.} \citet{caruana2004ensemble}
introduced forward selection with replacement from a model library, and
\citet{caruana2006getting} studied its overfitting and proposed bagged
selection. Stacked generalisation \citep{wolpert1992stacked} trains a
meta-learner on out-of-fold predictions; \citet{breiman1996stacked} showed the
value of constraining stacking weights to be non-negative. Auto-sklearn
\citep{feurer2015efficient} uses ensemble selection over models found by
Bayesian optimisation; AutoGluon-Tabular \citep{erickson2020autogluon} combines
repeated bagging, multi-layer stacking with skip connections to the raw
features, and a final weighted ensemble.

\paragraph{Cross-fitting.} Training on $K-1$ folds and predicting the held-out
fold to obtain honest nuisance estimates is standard in semiparametric
statistics \citep{chernozhukov2018double}; the same device underlies
out-of-fold predictions in stacking.

\paragraph{Calibration.} Temperature scaling \citep{guo2017calibration} is a
one-parameter post-hoc correction; \citet{niculescu2005predicting} documented
systematic miscalibration of boosted trees and other learners.

\paragraph{Tabular benchmarks.} OpenML-CC18 \citep{bischl2021openml} is a curated
suite of 72 classification tasks with fixed splits; the AutoML Benchmark
\citep{gijsbers2024amlb} compares AutoML systems under time budgets. Recent work
compares deep and tree-based models on tabular data
\citep{grinsztajn2022tree,mcelfresh2023neural}, and tabular foundation models
such as TabPFN \citep{hollmann2025tabpfn} are strong on small datasets.
We follow \citet{demsar2006statistical} and \citet{garcia2008extension} for
multi-dataset statistical comparison and \citet{dolan2002benchmarking} for
performance profiles.
